Lā ilāha illallāh waḥdahū lā sharīka lah

Morning & Evening Dhikr · The Tahlīl of Sovereignty & Praise

📖 Context of Recitation

This supreme declaration of Allāh’s oneness is to be recited ten times in the morning and ten times in the evening, or at least once when feeling lethargic. It serves as a protection from Shayṭān, a means of expiating sins, and a source of immense reward equivalent to freeing slaves.

📜 The Narration Chain & Context (Abū Hurayrah · Sahih Muslim)

Abū Hurayrah (ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn Ṣakhr) reported that Allah's Messenger (ﷺ) said:
"He who uttered these words: 'There is no god but Allah, the One, having no partner with Him. Sovereignty belongs to Him and all the praise is due to Him, and He is Potent over everything' one hundred times every day, there is a reward of emancipating ten slaves for him, and there are recorded hundred virtues to his credit, and hundred vices are blotted out from his scroll, and that is a safeguard for him against the Satan on that day till evening. And no one brings anything more excellent than this, except one who has done more than this. And he who utters: 'Exalted is Allah and all praise is due to Him' one hundred times a day, his sins are obliterated even if they are equal to the extent of the foam of the ocean."

✅ Source & Authenticity

The primary du'ā "Lā ilāha illallāhu waḥdahū..." is recorded in Hisn al-Muslim (92) from authentic narrations: An-Nasa'i, 'Amalul-Yawm wal-Laylah (no. 24), Ahmad (8/704, 16/293), Abu Dawud (4/319), and Ibn Majah (2/331). It is graded Sahih by Al-Albani (1/270, 1/272). The extended ḥadīth with both the Tahlīl (100x) and Tasbīḥ (100x) is Sahih Muslim 2691, forming a complete prophetic instruction for morning, evening, or any daily remembrance.

Abū Hurayrah (ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn Ṣakhr ad-Dawsī)

Full name: ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn Ṣakhr ibn Hindī al-Azdī ad-Dawsī al-Yamānī. He embraced Islam in 7 AH (629 CE) and became the most prolific narrator of ḥadīth (5,374 narrations). He died in 57 AH (678 CE) in Medina.

Renowned for his unwavering dedication to the Prophet (ﷺ), he spent years in the company of the Messenger, memorizing supplications, rulings, and prophetic teachings. His chains are considered among the most authentic via narrators like Mālik, Sumayy, and Abū Ṣāliḥ.

Role here: Direct narrator of this ḥadīth from the Prophet (ﷺ), transmitting the virtue of the Tahlīl and Tasbīḥ. The chain: Abū Hurayrah ← Abū Ṣāliḥ ← Sumayy ← Mālik ← Yaḥyā ibn Yaḥyā (in Muslim's recension).
