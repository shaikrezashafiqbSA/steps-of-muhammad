رَضِيتُ بِٱللَّٰهِ رَبًّا وَبِٱلْإِسْلَامِ دِينًا وَبِمُحَمَّدٍ نَبِيًّا

Raḍītu billāhi rabban wa bil-islāmi dīnan wa bi-Muḥammadin nabiyyan — “I am pleased with Allah as my Lord, with Islam as my religion, and with Muhammad as my Prophet.”

📖 Evening Declaration of Divine Pleasure

This powerful supplication was taught by the Prophet ﷺ to be recited every evening (after ‘Asr or at the onset of Maghrib). It expresses complete contentment with Allah’s Lordship, Islam as the way of life, and Muḥammad ﷺ as the final Messenger. The Prophet ﷺ guaranteed that whoever says these words with conviction in the evening, Allah makes it His duty to grant him pleasure and contentment.

📜 The Narration Chain & Context (Jāmi‘ at-Tirmidhī)

Thawbān (رضي الله عنه) reported that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said:
“Whoever says when he reaches the evening: ‘I am pleased with Allah as my Lord, with Islam as my religion, and with Muhammad as my Prophet,’ it is a duty upon Allah to please him.”

Thawbān ibn Bujdud (أبو عبد الله)

Full name: Thawbān (also spelled Thauban) ibn Bujdud, a freed slave (mawlā) of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ. He was originally from Yemen or from the tribe of Khawlān. He served the Prophet ﷺ closely and accompanied him on many journeys. He died in 54 AH (674 CE) in Hims (Homs), Syria, at the age of approximately 80.

Thawbān (ra) is known for his deep devotion and reliability. His narrations are found in the six major ḥadīth collections. Scholars regard him as a trustworthy Companion with a direct link to the Prophet ﷺ. In this chain, he reports directly from the Prophet ﷺ without intermediary, making the narration a high-probability transmission.

Role here: Thawbān transmits the very words of the Prophet ﷺ concerning the evening supplication, establishing a direct, prophetic source for this powerful duʿāʾ.

✅ Source & Authenticity

This ḥadīth is recorded by Imām Abū ʿĪsā Muḥammad at-Tirmidhī in his Jāmiʿ (Sunan at-Tirmidhī) under Book 48: Chapters on Supplication (Kitāb ad-Daʿawāt), Chapter 13: What has been related about supplication in the morning and evening. Imām Tirmidhī declared it Ḥasan Gharīb (sound and uncommon) via this chain. The narration is also supported by other Companions’ reports, making it a reliable and recommended dhikr for the evening. Scholars encourage its repetition as a means of attaining Allah’s pleasure and protection.
