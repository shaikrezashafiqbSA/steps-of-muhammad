Duʿāʾ al-Ṣabāḥ wa al-Masāʾ

Morning & Evening Supplications — Health, Protection, and Reliance on Allāh

📖 Context of Revelation & Practice

ʿAbdurRahmān ibn Abī Bakrah observed his father repeating specific supplications every morning and evening. Abū Bakrah (may Allāh be pleased with him) explained that he heard the Messenger of Allāh (ﷺ) using these very words, and he loved to follow his Sunnah. The narration also includes additional duʿās for refuge from disbelief, poverty, grave punishment, and a powerful prayer for the distressed.

📜 The Narration Chain & Context — Sunan Abī Dāwūd 5090

ʿAbdurRahmān ibn Abī Bakrah narrates from his father Abū Bakrah:
"ʿAbdurRahmān ibn Abī Bakrah said to his father: 'O my father! I hear you supplicating every morning: O Allah! Grant me health in my body. O Allah! Grant me good hearing. O Allah! Grant me good eyesight. There is no god but Thou.' You repeat them three times in the morning and three times in the evening. He said: I heard the Messenger of Allah (ﷺ) using these words as a supplication and I like to follow his practice."

Abū Bakrah (Nufayʿ ibn al-Ḥārith)

Full name: Nufayʿ ibn al-Ḥārith ibn Kaladah ath-Thaqafī, famously known as Abū Bakrah (may Allāh be pleased with him). He was a notable Companion who embraced Islam in the year of the conquest of Ṭāʾif (8 AH). He lived in Basra and narrated several ḥadīth. Died c. 51 AH.

Reliability: Abū Bakrah is considered a trustworthy narrator; his reports appear in the six major ḥadīth collections. He was known for his piety and direct transmission from the Prophet (ﷺ).

Role here: Abū Bakrah is the direct source of this duʿā practice; his son ʿAbdurRahmān heard him repeating the supplications and documented the chain, preserving the prophetic instruction for morning and evening invocations.

✅ Source & Authenticity

Recorded by Abū Dāwūd in Kitāb al-Adab, Bāb mā yaqūlu idhā aṣbaḥ (Book 43, Hadith 318). Shaykh al-Albānī graded it Ḥasan al-Isnād (good chain). The supplications are to be recited three times each morning and three times each evening, following the noble practice of the Prophet (ﷺ). The final duʿā (for the distressed) is also part of the same ḥadīth tradition and carries strong prophetic instruction.
