# Du'ā Upon Waking — The Refuge & End of Al-Ḥashr
### Morning & Evening Adhkār — Tirmidhi 2922

> **How to use:** Recite **three times** in the morning upon rising, and **three times** in the evening. Each cycle is: the refuge formula once, then the final three verses of Sūrat Al-Ḥashr.
>
> **Reward / virtue:** Allah appoints **seventy thousand angels** to send ṣalāt upon the reciter until the evening (or morning). If they die that day, they die a martyr.

---

## Section 1 · The Refuge Formula

> **Full meaning:** *I seek refuge with Allah, the All-Hearing, the All-Knowing, from the accursed Satan.*

## Section 2 · The Closing Verses of Sūrat Al-Ḥashr (Q. 59:22–24)

> **Note:** *The hadith instructs the reciter to read "three āyāt from the end of Sūrat Al-Ḥashr." These verses are reproduced here in full from the canonical reference for ease of recitation.*

> **Full meaning:** *He is Allah, besides whom there is no deity, the Knower of the unseen and the witnessed. He is the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful. He is Allah, besides whom there is no deity — the Sovereign, the Pure, the Source of Peace, the Granter of Security, the Overseer, the Almighty, the Compeller, the Supreme. Glory be to Allah, far above what they associate with Him. He is Allah — the Creator, the Originator, the Fashioner. To Him belong the most beautiful names. Whatever is in the heavens and the earth glorifies Him, and He is the Almighty, the Wise.*

## Section 3 · Hadith Context & Chain

<details>
<summary>📜 Read the full hadith narration & isnād</summary>

**Narrator:** Maʿqil ibn Yasār (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhu)

**English narration:** *"Whoever says three times when he gets up in the morning: 'Aʿūdhu billāhi-s-Samīʿi-l-ʿAlīm min ash-shayṭāni-r-rajīm' and then recites three āyāt from the end of Sūrat Al-Ḥashr — Allah appoints seventy thousand angels who send ṣalāt upon him until the evening. If he dies on that day, he dies a martyr. And whoever recites it in the evening holds the same status."*

**Arabic narration:**

> مَنْ قَالَ حِينَ يُصْبِحُ ثَلَاثَ مَرَّاتٍ: أَعُوذُ بِاللَّهِ السَّمِيعِ الْعَلِيمِ مِنَ الشَّيْطَانِ الرَّجِيمِ، وَقَرَأَ ثَلَاثَ آيَاتٍ مِنْ آخِرِ سُورَةِ الْحَشْرِ، وَكَّلَ اللَّهُ بِهِ سَبْعِينَ أَلْفَ مَلَكٍ يُصَلُّونَ عَلَيْهِ حَتَّى يُمْسِيَ، وَإِنْ مَاتَ فِي ذَلِكَ الْيَوْمِ مَاتَ شَهِيدًا، وَمَنْ قَالَهَا حِينَ يُمْسِي كَانَ بِتِلْكَ الْمَنْزِلَةِ.
>
> *Man qāla ḥīna yuṣbiḥu thalātha marrātin: aʿūdhu billāhi-s-samīʿi-l-ʿalīmi mina-sh-shayṭāni-r-rajīm, wa qaraʾa thalātha āyātin min ākhiri sūrati-l-ḥashr, wakkala-llāhu bihi sabʿīna alfa malakin yuṣallūna ʿalayhi ḥattā yumsī, wa in māta fī dhālika-l-yawmi māta shahīdan, wa man qālahā ḥīna yumsī kāna bi-tilka-l-manzilah.*

**Chain of transmission (isnād):**
- Maḥmūd ibn Ghaylān →
- Abū Aḥmad al-Zubayrī →
- Khālid ibn Ṭahmān Abū al-ʿAlāʾ al-Khaffāf →
- Nāfiʿ ibn Abī Nāfiʿ →
- Maʿqil ibn Yasār (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhu) →
- The Prophet ﷺ

**Compiler's note:** *Abū ʿĪsā (al-Tirmidhī) said: "This hadith is gharīb; we do not know it except from this route."*

## Section 4 · Source & Notes

**Reading instruction:** *Recite the refuge formula and the three verses **three times each** at morning (after Fajr) and at evening (after ʿAṣr or before Maghrib). The promised reward applies to both times.*

**Tradition source:** Jāmiʿ at-Tirmidhī, Book 45 (*Chapters on the Virtues of the Qur'ān*), Hadith 2922 (Book 42, Hadith 2922 in English numbering). **Grading: Ḍaʿīf** (graded weak by Darussalam due to the transmitter Khālid ibn Ṭahmān, whose memory was criticized in his later years). Al-Tirmidhī himself noted the hadith is *gharīb* (uniquely transmitted through this single chain). Despite the weak grading of the chain, the practice has been transmitted in the *adhkār* tradition by major scholars including Imām al-Nawawī in *al-Adhkār* and Ibn al-Sunnī in *ʿAmal al-Yawm wa-l-Laylah*, under the principle of *tasāhul* (leniency) applied to hadiths on virtues of recitation (*faḍāʾil al-aʿmāl*). The refuge formula itself (*aʿūdhu billāhi-s-Samīʿi-l-ʿAlīm...*) is supported by other authentic narrations in different contexts, and the three concluding verses of Sūrat Al-Ḥashr (Q. 59:22–24) are universally regarded as among the most exalted passages of the Qur'ān containing the divine names.
