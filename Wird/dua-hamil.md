# Doa untuk Hamil
### Prayers for Pregnancy — Complete Word-by-Word Guide

> **How to use:** A traditional compilation of supplications recited by Muslim women during pregnancy and childbirth. It contains three distinct prayers: a daily du'ā after every prayer, a du'ā with Sūrah At-Tāriq from the seventh month onward, and a du'ā with Qur'ānic healing verses for difficult labor.

---

## Section 1 · Du'ā After Every Prayer

**Instruction:** *Setiap lepas sholat sambil memegang pusar baca do'a ini.* — After every obligatory prayer, while gently placing the hand on the navel, recite this du'ā.

> **Full meaning:** *O Allah, if You have created a being in this womb of mine, then form it as a male, and shape it in a beautiful, complete form. Make him a righteous man — knowledgeable, wise, obedient to the Lord of Allah Most High, to His Messenger ﷺ, and to his parents. Make him among those granted lawful, pure, blessed, abundant provision. Protect him from the two disgraces — poverty and debt. Keep him safe from the calamities of this world and the next, and from their trials. O Allah, do not make him among the people of harm, and guide us to the straight path — the path of those You have favored, not of those who incurred Your anger nor of those who went astray. Āmīn, by Your mercy, O Most Merciful of the merciful. All praise is for Allah, Lord of the worlds. My Lord, do not leave me alone, for You are the best of inheritors.*

## Section 2 · Recitation from the 7th to 9th Month

**Instruction:** *Dari bulan ketujuh sampai sembilan, baca Surat al-Fātiḥah, lalu shalawat kepada Nabi (Allāhumma ṣalli ʿalā sayyidinā Muḥammad) tujuh kali atau lebih, lalu baca Surat At-Tāriq.* — From the 7th to the 9th month: recite Sūrah al-Fātiḥah, then ṣalawāt upon the Prophet ﷺ (*Allāhumma ṣalli ʿalā sayyidinā Muḥammad*) seven times or more, then recite Sūrah At-Tāriq up to verse 7. The reciter places her hands on her belly above the navel and slides them gently down toward the pelvic area while reciting.

### 2b · Du'ā for Safe Delivery (Recite 7 Times)

**Instruction:** *Sampai ayat (yakhruju min bayni-ṣ-ṣulbi wa-t-tarāʾib), lalu sambil memegang dengan dua tangan dari belakang punggung sampai depan perut sampai kemaluan, baca do'a ini 7 kali, kemudian lengkapi Surat At-Tāriq.* — After reaching the verse *"emerging from between the backbone and the ribs"*, place both hands at the lower back and sweep them gently from the back across the belly toward the pelvis. Recite this du'ā **seven times**, then complete the remainder of Sūrah At-Tāriq.

> **Full meaning:** *O Allah, bring forth this child from my womb through my birth-canal at the time of my delivery — safely and happily, in this world and the next. O Ḥannā who gave birth to Maryam, and Maryam who gave birth to ʿĪsā — come out, O newborn, by the power of the King who is worshipped. O Allah, accept my supplication just as You accepted the supplication of our master and intercessor Muḥammad ﷺ, by Your mercy, O Most Merciful of the merciful. All praise is for Allah, Lord of the worlds. O Allah, be gentle in easing every difficulty, and I ask You for ease and well-being in this world and the next.*

> ⚠️ **Note on authenticity:** The phrase *"Yā Ḥannā waladat Maryam, wa Maryam waladat ʿĪsā"* (calling on Hannah and Maryam) is a **folk supplication** popular in Yemeni/Ḥaḍramī and Southeast Asian midwifery traditions. It is **not from authentic hadith**, and direct address to deceased persons for assistance is contested by scholars. The safer practice is to address all petitions to Allah alone, omitting that line.

## Section 3 · Doa Terhalang Kelahiran (Du'ā for Difficult Labor)

**Instruction:** *Jika terhalang kelahiran perempuan, baca ayat-ayat dan Surat Al-Zalzalah dalam air, kemudian minum perempuan itu dan basuhkan ke perutnya — Insya-Allah dia akan melahirkan dengan mudah.* — If a woman has obstructed labor, recite these verses and Sūrah Al-Zalzalah over a vessel of water. Have the woman drink the water and wipe some of it onto her belly. She will deliver the child easily, God willing. This practice has been tested and proven beneficial by Allah's grace.

### 3e · Sūrah Al-Zalzalah (99:1–8) — The Closing Recitation

> **Full meaning:** *When the earth is shaken with its [final] earthquake, and the earth discharges its burdens, and man says, "What is with it?" — That Day it will report its news, because your Lord has commanded it. That Day people will depart separated to be shown the results of their deeds. So whoever does an atom's weight of good will see it, and whoever does an atom's weight of evil will see it.*

> 💡 **Why this surah:** Al-Zalzalah speaks of the earth "discharging its burdens" (*akhrajati-l-arḍu athqālahā*) — the practitioner draws a symbolic parallel to the womb releasing its precious burden in delivery.

## Section 4 · Source & Notes

**Reading instruction:** *Lengkapi Surat At-Tāriq selepas do'a kelahiran, dan baca semua ayat penyembuhan beserta Surat Al-Zalzalah ke dalam air sebelum diminum oleh perempuan yang sedang bersalin.* — Complete Sūrah At-Tāriq (verses 8–17) after the safe-delivery du'ā. For Section 3, recite all the healing verses and Sūrah Al-Zalzalah over a vessel of water, then have the laboring woman drink from it and wipe some onto her belly.

**Tradition source:** This compilation is preserved in Malay/Indonesian *kitab kuning* and in Ḥaḍramī Yemeni midwifery manuals. The composed du'ās in Sections 1 and 2b are pious devotional supplications and are **not directly traced to canonical hadith** (Bukhārī, Muslim, the Four Sunan, etc.). The Qur'ānic verses embedded throughout — Sūrah At-Tāriq (Q. 86), Ar-Raʿd 13:8, Fāṭir 35:11, An-Naḥl 16:78 and 16:69, At-Tawbah 9:14, Yūnus 10:57, Al-Isrāʾ 17:82, Ash-Shuʿarāʾ 26:80, Fuṣṣilat 41:44, Al-Zalzalah (Q. 99), and the closing line of Section 1 (Q. 21:89) — are fully authentic revelation. The invocation *"Yā Ḥannā waladat Maryam..."* in Section 2b is a folk supplication and is theologically contested; the safer practice is to address all petitions directly to Allah. Asking specifically for a male child in Section 1 differs from the etiquette of Sūrah ash-Shūrā (42:49–50), which teaches gratitude for whatever Allah grants — reciters may omit *dhakaran* and leave the gender to Allah's wisdom.
