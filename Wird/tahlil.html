Tahlīl al‑Arwāḥ

A Comprehensive Guidebook to Tahlil Arwah – From Origins to Every Word

📜 Part 1 · Origins – Where Does Tahlil Come From?

What Does “Tahlil” Mean? The word tahlīl (تَهْلِيل) comes from the Arabic root h-l-l, literally meaning the act of saying Lā ilāha illa Allāh – “There is no god but Allah.” In the Malay‑Muslim world (Singapore, Malaysia, Indonesia, Brunei), Tahlil Arwah is a communal gathering where the Qurʾān is recited, ṣalawāt are sent upon the Prophet ﷺ, and duʿā is made for the deceased.

The Wali Songo – the Nine Saints of Java (15th–16th centuries) – structured this practice by retaining pre‑Islamic gathering forms (7th, 40th, 100th day) but replacing non‑Islamic content with Qurʾān, dhikr, and prayers. Later, scholars like Imam Sayyid ʿAbdullāh bin ʿAlawī al‑Ḥaddād (d. 1720 CE) formalised the sequence, making tahlil a blend of prophetic teaching, scholarly refinement, and local wisdom.

Is Tahlil Permitted? Yes. Imam An‑Nawawī affirmed that the reward of Qurʾān recitation reaches the deceased through duʿā. The Qurʾān commands praying for those who preceded us in faith (59:10), and the Prophet ﷺ said: “No people sit remembering Allah but that the angels surround them…” (Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 2700). Charity on behalf of the deceased is also established (Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 1004).

💡 Part 2 · Rationale – Why Do We Perform Tahlil?

Spiritual benefits: Renewal of faith (tawḥīd), sending reward to the deceased, and following the Sunnah (reciting Sūrah Yāsīn for the dying and the dead).

Psychological & communal: Comfort for the grieving, strengthening family bonds, and educating the young.

Why Sūrah Yāsīn? The Prophet ﷺ said: “Everything has a heart, and the heart of the Qurʾān is Yā Sīn.” (Sunan al‑Tirmidhī) Imām al‑Ghazālī explained that it affirms resurrection and the afterlife – the very realities the deceased have entered.

Part 3 · The Basic Structure of a Tahlil Gathering
📋 Sequence (core steps)
  1. Opening with Sūrah al‑Fātiḥah (dedications to Prophet ﷺ, family, companions)
  2. Shahādah (testimony of faith) – 3 times
  3. First tahlīl sequence – 4 declarations of Tawḥīd
  4. Second tahlīl sequence – 9 praises of Allah
  5. Istighfār (seeking forgiveness) including Sayyid al‑Istighfār
  6. Dedication of al‑Fātiḥah to the Prophet, saints, teachers, and all deceased
  7. Ṣalawāt – Yā Rabbī ṣalli ʿalā Muḥammad (23 verses)
  8. Qurʾānic prelude (Sūrah al‑Fatḥ 48:1‑3 & Sūrah al‑Tawbah 9:128‑129)
  9. Recitation of Sūrah Yāsīn (entire chapter 36)
  10. Closing ṣalawāt: al‑Mubīn, Nāriyyah, Bushrā
  11. Final duʿā for the deceased and living, Sūrah al‑ʿAṣr, and Tasbīḥ Kaffārah

🔹 Section 3 – First Tahlīl Sequence: Four Declarations of Tawḥīd
Declaration 1: There is no god but Allah, alone, with no partner. To Him belongs all dominion and all praise. He gives life and causes death. He is the Ever‑Living who never dies. In His hand is all good, and He has power over all things.
لَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا اللهُ وَحْدَهُ لَا شَرِيكَ لَهُ، لَهُ الْمُلْكُ وَلَهُ الْحَمْدُ يُحْيِي وَيُمِيتُ وَهُوَ حَيٌّ لَا يَمُوتُ بِيَدِهِ الْخَيْرُ وَهُوَ عَلَى كُلِّ شَيْءٍ قَدِيرٌNone has the right to be worshipped except Allah aloneLā ilāha illallāh, waḥdahū lā sharīka lah…
Declaration 2: I bear witness… One God, the Eternal, who has taken neither a wife nor a child.
أَشْهَدُ أَنْ لَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا اللهُ وَحْدَهُ لَا شَرِيكَ لَهُ، إِلَهًا وَاحِدًا أَحَدًا صَمَدًا لَمْ يَتَّخِذْ صَاحِبَةً وَلَا وَلَدًاI bear witness… One God, the Eternal RefugeAshhadu an lā ilāha illallāh… ilāhan wāḥidan aḥadan ṣamadā

Part 5 · Understanding Sūrah Yāsīn – The Heart of the Tahlil
📖 Why Is It Called the Heart of the Qurʾān?

The Prophet ﷺ said: “Everything has a heart, and the heart of the Qurʾān is Yā Sīn.” (Sunan al‑Tirmidhī) Imām al‑Ghazālī explained that the validity of faith lies in the acknowledgement of resurrection and the afterlife, which this sūrah affirms most eloquently.

Key themes: Divine message (1‑12), story of the messengers (13‑32), signs of Allah’s power (33‑50), the Day of Judgement (51‑70), and Allah’s absolute power (71‑83).

When recited in tahlil: at the time of death (Ibn Ḥibbān, Ṣaḥīḥ), for seven days after burial (tradition of Makkah and Madīnah), and as the primary Qurʾānic offering in tahlil gatherings.

Part 6 · The Deeper Meaning of the Tahlil Gathering
🌿 Spiritual, Communal & Psychological Benefits

Spiritual: Renewing tawḥīd, sending continuous reward to the deceased, and the Prophet’s ﷺ love for his community (he said: “Whoever sends blessings upon me, I am informed of it”).

Communal: Grief shared is grief halved; intergenerational connection; charity through food (ṣadaqah) on behalf of the deceased.

Balanced view on bidʿah: The acts (Qurʾān, dhikr, duʿā) are firmly established. The format is a cultural vehicle – the Wali Songo gave structure to existing worship. The majority of Sunni scholars accept tahlil as permissible and commendable. As Ustaz Ahmad Khalil notes: “These practices are common and are not merely cultural customs. Their foundations are deeply rooted in the Sharīʿah.”

🤲 Conclusion & Duʿā

Tahlil Arwah is not a ritual to be performed on autopilot. Every phrase is a theological statement, a supplication, or an act of communal love. Understanding what you are saying transforms attendance into genuine worship.

May Allah accept from all of us, elevate the ranks of our deceased, and gather us all in His mercy and light. Āmīn.
