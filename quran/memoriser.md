## Doa Mudah Penghafal — The Memorizer’s Supplication for Cognitive Clarity, Knowledge, and Character
<details>
<summary>📚 Introduction </summary>

**Nusantara Scholastic Context:**
This beautiful compilation is classically transmitted as an *ijazah* (scholarly authorization) through prominent traditional teachers, notably traced here through **Al-Habib Prof. Dr. K.H. Said Agil Husin Al Munawwar** (former Minister of Religious Affairs for Indonesia, a world-renowned master of Qira'at, Quranic sciences, and Hadith). In Singaporean and regional madrasahs, it is traditionally recited three times after Solat Asar to invoke divine aid in retaining sacred knowledge.

*Note on Text Adjustments:* To help your reading, we have corrected a few common phonetic spelling variations found in raw regional texts (e.g., converting "ifzol" to the correct Arabic *hifẓ*, "takhrimni" to *akrimnī*, and "jamiilni" to *jammilnī*).

### 📜 The Supplication

#### 👤 1. Singular Form (Perseorangan — Recited 3x)
> *"O Allah! I ask You for the understanding of the Prophets, the retention of the Messengers, and the intuitive inspiration of the angels brought near [to You]. O Allah! Enrich me with knowledge, adorn me with forbearance, honor me with piety, and beautify me with well-being. By Your mercy, O Most Merciful of those who show mercy."*

#### 👥 2. Plural Form (Jema'ah — Recited 3x)
> *"O Allah! We ask You for the understanding of the Prophets, the retention of the Messengers, and the intuitive inspiration of the angels brought near [to You]. O Allah! Enrich us with knowledge, adorn us with forbearance, honor us with piety, and beautify us with well-being. By Your mercy, O Most Merciful of those who show mercy."*

### 👥 Plural Adjustments (For Congregational Recitation)
When leading a group or reciting together, swap the singular pronouns with the plural forms as follows:
*   Instead of *Innī* (إِنِّي) Use **Inna** (إِنَّا - *Indeed we*)
*   Instead of *As'aluka* (أَسْأَلُكَ) Use **Nas'aluka** (نَسْأَلُكَ - *We ask You*)
*   Instead of *Aghninī* (أَغْنِنِي) Use **Aghninā** (أَغْنِنَا - *Enrich us*)
*   Instead of *Zayyinnī* (زَيِّنِّي) Use **Zayyinnā** (زَيِّنَّا - *Adorn us*)
*   Instead of *Akrimnī* (أَكْرِمْنِي) Use **Akrimnā** (أَكْرِمْنَا - *Honor us*)
*   Instead of *Jammilnī* (جَمِّلْنِي)  Use **Jammilnā** (جَمِّلْنَا - *Beautify us*)

<summary>📚 Technical Source Analysis & Scholarly References</summary>

This supplication is composed of two distinct historical segments seamlessly merged by classical scholars of Islamic pedagogy to form an ultimate student's formula:

### Analysis of Part 1 (Fahman-Nabiyyin...)
*   **Source Status:** This specific phrasing is categorized as a **Du'a Ma'thur/Mashhur**—a supplication popularized by the early righteous generations (*Salaf al-Salih*) and Sufi masters rather than a direct prophetic text.
*   **Literature Presence:** It is highly visible in standard classical curricula regarding the etiquette of seeking knowledge (*Adab al-Ta'allum*). You will find versions of it routinely referenced in commentaries of Islamic learning manuals used across Islamic institutions globally.

### Analysis of Part 2 (Allahumma Aghnini bil-'Ilmi...)
*   **Hadith Citations:** This specific segment is rooted in the early narrative text tradition. It was recorded by:
    1.  **Al-Khatib al-Baghdadi** in his monumental historical encyclopedia, *Tarikh Baghdad* (Vol. 5, p. 468).
    2.  **Al-Daylami** in *Musnad al-Firdaws* (No. 1285) via a chain tracing back to the Companion Abdullah ibn Umar (رضي الله عنهما).
    3.  **Imam al-Ghazali** highlights and recommends it within the *Ihya Ulum al-Din* (The Revival of the Religious Sciences) under the internal spiritual disciplines of acquisition.
*   **Hadith Classification:** Hadith critical masters (like Al-Iraqi and Ibn Hajar) classify the explicit Prophetic attribution chain of this specific text segment as **Da'if** (weak). However, its theological concepts are entirely sound (*Sahih al-Ma'na*), aligning perfectly with authentic Quranic objectives. Consequently, it remains standard across all Sunni legal and spiritual schools for practice within *Fada'il al-A'mal* (virtuous behaviors).
