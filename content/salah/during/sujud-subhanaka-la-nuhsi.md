# Seeking Refuge in Allah's Pleasure from His Anger — The Prophet's ﷺ Sajdah Supplication

## 📜 The Hadith

ʿĀʾishah ؓ reported: One night I missed Allah's Messenger ﷺ from the bed, and when I sought him, my hand touched the soles of his feet while he was in the state of prostration; they (his feet) were raised upright, and he was saying: *"O Allah, I seek refuge in Thy pleasure from Thy anger, and in Thy forgiveness from Thy punishment, and I seek refuge in Thee from Thee (Thy anger). I cannot reckon Thy praise. Thou art as Thou hast lauded Thyself."*

<details>
<summary>۞ الْحِكْمَة — Divine Wisdom</summary>

> **Source: Textual.** This reading follows from the wording of the text itself.

The supplication is built on a striking rhetorical progression: it first seeks refuge in something of Allah — [[ بِرِضَاكَ | in Your pleasure | bi-riḍāka ]] and [[ وَبِمُعَافَاتِكَ | and in Your forgiveness | wa-bi-muʿāfātika ]] — against something else of Allah, and then collapses the distinction entirely: [[ بِكَ | in You | bika ]] [[ مِنْكَ | from You | minka ]]. The text moves from refuge in His attributes to refuge in His Essence, as though teaching that no attribute alone is a shield except Allah Himself.

The second half performs the same movement in praise rather than refuge. The speaker does not say "I praise You greatly" but [[ لَا | not | lā ]] [[ أُحْصِي | I can reckon | uḥṣī ]] [[ ثَنَاءً | praise | thanā'an ]] — an admission of incapacity — and then delegates the standard itself back to Allah: [[ أَنْتَ | You are | anta ]] [[ كَمَا | as | kamā ]] [[ أَثْنَيْتَ | You have praised | athnayta ]] [[ عَلَى | upon | ʿalā ]] [[ نَفْسِكَ | Yourself | nafsika ]]. Human praise is measured, found insufficient, and replaced by the only adequate praise: the praise Allah spoke over Himself.

The narrative frame underscores the lesson: the Prophet ﷺ utters these words in sujūd, the posture of utmost humility, and ʿĀʾishah ؓ finds him not in bed but in the mosque at night — the moment of refuge sought and praise reckoned happens in the lowest physical position a person can take.

</details>
