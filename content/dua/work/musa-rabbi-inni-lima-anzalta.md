# The Supplication of Mūsā at Madyan

## 📜 The Qur'anic Verse


Exhausted, homeless, and fleeing for his life from the tyrants of Egypt, Mūsā ؑ arrived in the land of Madyan with absolutely nothing. Yet, after selflessly spending his last ounces of strength to water the flocks for two stranger women, he did not complain or demand a reward. 

Instead, he withdrew to the shade of a tree, showcasing the ultimate etiquette of supplication—refusing to beg demandingly, but rather presenting his brokenness as a declaration of love and absolute trust in God's decree. He turned to Allah and whispered a masterpiece of dignified gratitude:

This quiet admission of absolute dependency (Iftiqār) became the precise key that unlocked his future. He did not ask for a house, a wage, or a wife; he simply declared himself desperate for whatever goodness Allah chose to send down. 

In response to this beautiful vulnerability, Allah answered him almost instantly—transforming a stranded refugee into a man with an honorable career, a safe home, and a righteous family all in a single afternoon.
