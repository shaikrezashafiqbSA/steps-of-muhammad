# The Duʿāʾ of Ādam and Ḥawwāʾ — The First Repentance

## 📜 The Qur'anic Verse

After they ate from the forbidden tree and their shame became manifest to them, Ādam and Ḥawwāʾ ؑ turned to Allah in the first repentance of humanity: *"Our Lord! We have wronged ourselves. If You do not forgive us and have mercy on us, we will certainly be losers."* (7:23)

## الضَّمَان ۞ Allah's Guarantee

The verse binds loss to the *absence* of forgiveness and mercy — by inversion, forgiveness and mercy are the condition of salvation, a condition Allah answered in 7:23's sequel: *"Then his Lord chose him and turned to him in forgiveness and guided him"* (2:37, 20:122).

<details>
<summary>۞ الْحِكْمَة — Divine Wisdom</summary>

> **Source: Commentary.** Attributed to Tafsīr al-Qurʾān al-ʿAẓīm, Ibn Kathīr ؒ.

Ibn Kathīr ؒ records from Qatādah ؒ that Ādam ؑ asked, "O Lord! What if I repented and sought forgiveness?" and Allah answered, "Then I will admit you into Paradise." By contrast, Shayṭān asked not for forgiveness but for respite — and each was given exactly what he asked for. The wisdom of the verse lies in this contrast: the children of Ādam inherit a father whose response to sin was confession — [[ ظَلَمْنَا | we have wronged | ẓalamnā ]] [[ أَنْفُسَنَا | ourselves | anfusanā ]] — not excuse, while their enemy's response was pride. Ad-Dahhāk ؒ adds that these very words are "the words that Adam received from his Lord," linking this āyah to [[ فَتَلَقَّىٰ | then he received | fatalaqqā ]] [[ آدَمُ | Ādam | Ādamu ]] [[ مِنْ | from | min ]] [[ رَبِّهِ | his Lord | rabbihī ]] [[ كَلِمَاتٍ | words | kalimātin ]] (2:37). The duʿāʾ is thus not merely Adam's — it is divinely taught, making it the prophetic template for every sinner's return.

</details>
