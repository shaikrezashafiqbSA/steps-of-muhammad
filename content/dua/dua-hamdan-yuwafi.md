# دُعَاءُ الْحَمْد — The Supplication of Praise

## 📜 The Supplication

This comprehensive duʿāʾ opens with the praise of Allah, followed by abundant ṣalawāt upon the Prophet ﷺ, then a series of petitions for protection, well-being, and steadfastness. It is recited in gatherings of dhikr and after obligatory prayers, and most famously after the recitation of Sūrat Yāsīn, in the tradition of the Ba ʿAlawī scholars of Ḥaḍramawt, as transmitted through the Ḥabāʾib, including Ḥabīb ʿAbdullāh bin ʿAlawī al-Ḥaddād and the chains connected to Ḥabīb ʿAlī al-Ḥabashī and Ḥabīb ʿUmar bin Ḥafīẓ.

The worshipper begins: *"All praise is for Allah, Lord of all the worlds, praise that fulfills His favours and is commensurate with His increase. O our Lord, for You is all praise as befits the majesty of Your Face and the greatness of Your dominion. Glory be to You, we cannot enumerate praise upon You; You are as You have praised Yourself..."* The worshipper then sends blessings upon the Prophet ﷺ among the first and the last, entrusts to Allah his religion, soul, family and wealth, seeks His shelter from every rebellious devil, obstinate tyrant, evil eye, wrongdoing and envy, asks to be adorned with well-being and steadfastness, and seals with the Qur'anic verses of Sūrat aṣ-Ṣāffāt.

<details>
<summary>📚 Original Sources of the Composite Text </summary>

**No single isnād.** This is a compiled duʿāʾ, not one hadith. Its recited phrases trace to the following sources:

**1 · Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 486 — narrated by ʿĀʾishah (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhā):**
[[ لَا أُحْصِي ثَنَاءً عَلَيْكَ | I cannot enumerate praise upon You | lā uḥṣī thanāʾan ʿalayk ]]
[[ أَنْتَ كَمَا أَثْنَيْتَ عَلَى نَفْسِكَ | You are as You have praised Yourself | anta kamā athnayta ʿalā nafsik ]]
The Prophet ﷺ said this in prostration during the night prayer. **Grade: Ṣaḥīḥ.**

**2 · Sunan Ibn Mājah 3801 — narrated by ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿUmar (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhumā):**
[[ يَا رَبِّ لَكَ الْحَمْدُ كَمَا يَنْبَغِي لِجَلَالِ وَجْهِكَ وَلِعَظِيمِ سُلْطَانِكَ | O Lord, for You is praise as befits the majesty of Your Face and the greatness of Your dominion | yā Rabbi laka-l-ḥamdu kamā yanbaghī li-jalāli wajhika wa li-ʿaẓīmi sulṭānik ]]
A servant of Allah said this; the two recording angels could not encompass how to record it and ascended to ask their Lord, who said: *"Write it as My slave said it, until he meets Me and I reward him for it."* **Grade: Ḍaʿīf isnād** (al-Albānī; Islamweb Fatwā 65961), though widely recited.

**3 · "Ḥamdan yuwāfī niʿamahu wa yukāfiʾu mazīdah":**
Not an established Prophetic hadith. Reported (via Abū Naṣr at-Tammār) as words taught to Ādam (ʿalayhi-s-salām), an Isrāʾīlī report, as Ibn al-Qayyim notes in *ʿUddat aṣ-Ṣābirīn*. It is nevertheless regarded in the Shāfiʿī school as among the most sublime formulas of ḥamd, which is why it features across the awrād of the Ba ʿAlawī tradition.

**4 · "Nastaḥfiẓuka wa nastawdiʿuka..." (Parts 7–8):**
Transmitted in the duʿāʾ recited after Sūrat Yāsīn in the tradition of the Ḥabāʾib, commonly attributed to Ḥabīb ʿAbdullāh bin ʿAlawī al-Ḥaddād. Its theme echoes the authentic Prophetic farewell duʿāʾ "astawdiʿu-llāha dīnaka wa amānatak..." (Abū Dāwūd, at-Tirmidhī).

**5 · Qur'anic Seal — Sūrat aṣ-Ṣāffāt 37:180–182:**
[[ سُبْحَانَ رَبِّكَ رَبِّ الْعِزَّةِ عَمَّا يَصِفُونَ | Glory be to your Lord, the Lord of Honour, above what they describe | subḥāna Rabbika Rabbi-l-ʿizzati ʿammā yaṣifūn ]]
[[ وَسَلَامٌ عَلَى الْمُرْسَلِينَ | and peace be upon the Messengers | wa salāmun ʿala-l-mursalīn ]]
[[ وَالْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ | and all praise is for Allah, Lord of the worlds | wa-l-ḥamdu lillāhi Rabbi-l-ʿālamīn ]]
**Grade: Qur'anic Scripture.**

</details>

<details>
<summary>🔎 Hadith/Quran source of the line above </summary>

**Qur'an, Sūrat al-Fātiḥah 1:1–2.** These two lines are the opening verses of the Qur'an verbatim.

> بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ ٱلرَّحْمَـٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ ﴿١﴾ ٱلْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ ٱلْعَـٰلَمِينَ ﴿٢﴾

**Grade: Qur'anic Scripture.**

<details>
<summary>⚡ The Divine Guarantee within this source</summary>

Sūrat al-Fātiḥah carries no reward clause within these two verses themselves. The promise attached to the Sūrah is stated in a separate ḥadīth qudsī (Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 395), where Allah divides the prayer between Himself and His servant:

[[ حَمِدَنِي | "My servant has praised Me." | ḥamidanī ]] [[ عَبْدِي | (lit. My servant) | ʿabdī ]]

*Accuracy note: Muslim 395 is the number I have for this narration. Verify it before citing.*

<details>
<summary>🔎 Hadith/Quran source of the line above </summary>

**No Prophetic source.** This formula has no established marfūʿ isnād. Ibn al-Qayyim, in *ʿUddat aṣ-Ṣābirīn*, records it as an Isrāʾīlī report, transmitted via Abū Naṣr at-Tammār, describing words taught to Ādam (ʿalayhi-s-salām). As-Saffārīnī cites it similarly in *Ghidhāʾ al-Albāb*.

I cannot supply a raw hadith text for this line, because no authenticated Prophetic narration containing it has been established. The Shāfiʿī school nonetheless treats it as among the highest formulas of ḥamd, which explains its place in the Ba ʿAlawī awrād. Ibn al-Qayyim and Bakr Abū Zayd objected to its use.

### Part 4 · Glory Beyond All Enumeration

[[ سُبْحَانَكَ | glory be to You, | subḥānaka ]] [[ لَا | not | lā ]] [[ نُحْصِي | can we enumerate | nuḥṣī ]] [[ ثَنَاءً | praise | thanāʾan ]] [[ عَلَيْكَ | upon You; | ʿalayk ]]

[[ أَنْتَ | You are | anta ]] [[ كَمَا | as | kamā ]] [[ أَثْنَيْتَ | You have praised | athnayta ]] [[ عَلَى | upon | ʿalā ]] [[ نَفْسِكَ | Yourself. | nafsik ]]

<details>
<summary>🔎 Hadith/Quran source of the line above </summary>

**Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 486**, Book of Prayer, narrated by ʿĀʾishah (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhā).

> عَنْ عَائِشَةَ، قَالَتْ: فَقَدْتُ رَسُولَ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم لَيْلَةً مِنَ الْفِرَاشِ، فَالْتَمَسْتُهُ فَوَقَعَتْ يَدِي عَلَى بَطْنِ قَدَمَيْهِ وَهُوَ فِي الْمَسْجِدِ وَهُمَا مَنْصُوبَتَانِ، وَهُوَ يَقُولُ: اللَّهُمَّ أَعُوذُ بِرِضَاكَ مِنْ سَخَطِكَ، وَبِمُعَافَاتِكَ مِنْ عُقُوبَتِكَ، وَأَعُوذُ بِكَ مِنْكَ، لَا أُحْصِي ثَنَاءً عَلَيْكَ، أَنْتَ كَمَا أَثْنَيْتَ عَلَى نَفْسِكَ.

*Translation:* ʿĀʾishah said: I missed the Messenger of Allah ﷺ from the bed one night. I searched for him and my hand fell upon the soles of his feet while he was in the place of prayer, and they were upright, and he was saying: "O Allah, I seek refuge in Your pleasure from Your displeasure, and in Your pardon from Your punishment, and I seek refuge in You from You. I cannot enumerate praise upon You; You are as You have praised Yourself."

**Grade: Ṣaḥīḥ.**

*Note on wording:* the Prophet ﷺ spoke in the singular (*lā uḥṣī*, "I cannot enumerate"). The duʿāʾ as recited in the Ba ʿAlawī awrād shifts this to the congregational plural (*lā nuḥṣī*, "we cannot enumerate") and prefixes *subḥānaka*, which is not in the Muslim text.

*Accuracy note: this Arabic is reproduced from memory. Verify against sunnah.com before publication.*

<summary>🔎 Hadith/Quran source of the line above </summary>

**The closing two lines are Qur'anic in origin.** The ṣalawāt formulas themselves ("fi-l-awwalīn wa-l-ākhirīn") are compositions of the awrād tradition, not a quoted hadith text. The inheritance wording draws on two verses:

**Qur'an, Sūrat Maryam 19:40:**
> إِنَّا نَحْنُ نَرِثُ ٱلْأَرْضَ وَمَنْ عَلَيْهَا وَإِلَيْنَا يُرْجَعُونَ

*Indeed, it is We who will inherit the earth and whoever is upon it, and to Us they will be returned.*

**Qur'an, Sūrat al-Anbiyāʾ 21:89 (the duʿāʾ of Zakariyyā, ʿalayhi-s-salām):**
> رَبِّ لَا تَذَرْنِى فَرْدًۭا وَأَنتَ خَيْرُ ٱلْوَٰرِثِينَ

*My Lord, do not leave me alone, and You are the best of inheritors.*

**Grade: Qur'anic Scripture.**

*Note:* the duʿāʾ adapts these phrases; it does not quote either verse verbatim.

<summary>🔎 Hadith/Quran source of the line above </summary>

The duʿāʾ text above is not this hadith. It expands the same idea into a congregational plural and adds wealth and family to the list of entrusted things.

## ✅ Source & Authenticity

This supplication is a **compiled duʿāʾ** recited in the gatherings (majālis) of the Ba ʿAlawī tradition of Ḥaḍramawt as transmitted by the scholars of the Ḥabāʾib. It is **not a single hadith** and has no single isnād. Verified breakdown of its components:

- **"Lā nuḥṣī thanāʾan ʿalayk, anta kamā athnayta ʿalā nafsik"** is authentically established: **Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 486**, narrated by ʿĀʾishah (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhā), from the Prophet's ﷺ words in prostration. The awrād recite it in the plural; the hadith is in the singular.
- **"Laka-l-ḥamd kamā yanbaghī li-jalāli wajhika wa ʿaẓīmi sulṭānik"** is **Sunan Ibn Mājah 3801**, narrated by ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿUmar (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhumā), with the reward-narrative of the two angels. Its isnād is graded **ḍaʿīf** (al-Albānī; cf. Islamweb Fatāwā 65961 and 105601), though the wording remains widely recited as duʿāʾ.
- **"Ḥamdan yuwāfī niʿamahu wa yukāfiʾu mazīdah"** has **no established Prophetic isnād**. Ibn al-Qayyim (*ʿUddat aṣ-Ṣābirīn*) identifies it as an Isrāʾīlī report concerning Ādam (ʿalayhi-s-salām), transmitted via Abū Naṣr at-Tammār (cf. as-Saffārīnī, *Ghidhāʾ al-Albāb*). The Shāfiʿī school nonetheless regards it as among the most sublime formulas of ḥamd, which explains its prominence in Ba ʿAlawī awrād. Some scholars, including Ibn al-Qayyim and Bakr Abū Zayd, objected to it.
- **"Fa-laka-l-ḥamd ḥattā tarḍā..."** is a well-known formula of the awrād. I could not confirm a specific authenticated Prophetic isnād for this exact tripartite wording, so treat it as a transmitted devotional formula rather than a marfūʿ hadith.
- **Parts 7–9 ("nastaḥfiẓuka wa nastawdiʿuka...")** are transmitted in the duʿāʾ recited after Sūrat Yāsīn in the tradition of the Ḥabāʾib, commonly attributed to **Ḥabīb ʿAbdullāh bin ʿAlawī al-Ḥaddād**. They are thematically rooted in the authentic Prophetic entrustment duʿāʾ ("astawdiʿu-llāha dīnaka wa amānatak...").
- **Part 11** is **Qur'an, Sūrat aṣ-Ṣāffāt 37:180–182**.
- **Attribution note:** the compilation circulates in the majālis connected to Ḥabīb ʿAlī al-Ḥabashī and Ḥabīb ʿUmar bin Ḥafīẓ. A specific published citation for this exact compiled sequence under Ḥabīb ʿUmar's name could not be independently verified. Treat the attribution as traditional transmission rather than a documented text reference.
