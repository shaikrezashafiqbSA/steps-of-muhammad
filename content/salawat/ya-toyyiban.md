# The Longing of the Pilgrim for Ṭaybah

## 📜 The Qaṣīdah

The poet addresses Madinah by the name the Prophet ﷺ gave it, calling out *O Ṭaybah, O medicine of the gravely ill, we have longed for you and love itself has summoned us*. He describes watching the pilgrim ship depart without him, his tears refusing to dry, and his heart carried away with those who sailed. He turns to the Kaʿbah and asks *my qiblah, the House of Allah, I wait patiently; perhaps one day I shall be your visitor*. He then names his dearest wish: *my Prophet, my most precious hope is to visit you even once in my life, to pray my prayer beside you, to remember my Lord and recite the Qurʾān*. He closes by congratulating the city itself: *glad tidings to you, O Madinah, on the arrival of the Guide; have I any shelter within your sanctuary?*

**The Text (الْمَتْن)**

[[ يَا طَيْبَةْ يَا طَيْبَةْ | O Ṭaybah, O Ṭaybah | yā ṭaybah yā ṭaybah ]]
[[ يَا دَوَا الْعَيَانَا | O medicine of the gravely ill, | yā dawā l-ʿayyānā ]]
[[ اِشْتَقْنَالِكْ وَالْهَوَى نَادَانَا | we have longed for you, and love has called us. | ishtaqnā-lik wa-l-hawā nādānā ]]

[[ لَمَّا سَارَ الْمَرْكَبْ نَسَانِي | When the ship set sail, it left me behind. | lammā sāra l-markab nasānī ]]
[[ سَارُوا وَالدَّمْعُ مَا جَفَانِي | They sailed away, and my tears never deserted me. | sārū wa-d-damʿu mā jafānī ]]
[[ أَخَذُوا قَلْبِي مَعَ جَنَانِي | They took my heart along with my soul. | akhadhū qalbī maʿa janānī ]]
[[ يَا طَيْبَةْ يَا تِيمَ الْوَلْهَانَةْ | O Ṭaybah, O captor of the love-stricken. | yā ṭaybah yā tīma l-walhānah ]]

[[ قِبْلَتِي بَيْتُ اللهِ صَابِرْ | My qiblah, the House of Allah, I wait in patience. | qiblatī baytu llāhi ṣābir ]]
[[ عَلَّنِي يَوْمًا لَكِ زَائِرْ | Perhaps one day I shall be your visitor. | ʿallanī yawman laki zāʾir ]]
[[ يَا تُرَى هَلْ تَرَانِي نَاظِرْ لِلْكَعْبَةْ | I wonder, will it see me gazing upon the Kaʿbah, | yā turā hal tarānī nāẓir li-l-kaʿbah ]]
[[ وَتَغْمُرْنِي بِأَمَانَا | and will it envelop me in safety? | wa-taghmurnī bi-amānā ]]

[[ نَبِيَّنَا أَغْلَى أُمْنِيَاتِي | Our Prophet, my most precious wish | nabiyyanā aghlā umniyyātī ]]
[[ أَزُورَكْ وَلَوْ مَرَّةْ بِحَيَاتِي | is to visit you, even once in my life, | azūrak wa-law marrah bi-ḥayātī ]]
[[ وَبِجِوَارَكْ صَلِّي صَلَاتِي | and beside you to pray my prayer, | wa-bi-jiwārak ṣallī ṣalātī ]]
[[ وَأَذْكُرْ رَبِّي وَأَتْلُو الْقُرْآنَا | to remember my Lord and recite the Qurʾān. | wa-adhkur rabbī wa-atlu l-qurʾānā ]]

[[ بُشْرَاكِ الْمَدِينَةْ بُشْرَاكِ | Glad tidings to you, O Madinah, glad tidings, | bushrāki l-madīnah bushrāki ]]
[[ بِقُدُومِ الْهَادِي يَا بُشْرَاكِ | at the coming of the Guide, glad tidings to you. | bi-qudūmi l-hādī yā bushrāki ]]
[[ وَهَلْ لِي مَأْوًى فِي حِمَاكِ | Have I any shelter within your sanctuary? | wa-hal lī maʾwan fī ḥimāki ]]
[[ أَتَمَنَّى فَالنُّورُ سَبَانَا | I yearn for it, for your light has captivated us. | atamannā fa-n-nūru sabānā ]]

## Notes for a Shāfiʿī reading

**On the name Ṭaybah.** Sahih Muslim records that Allah named the city Ṭābah, at approximately Muslim 1385. Sahih al-Bukhari records the Prophet ﷺ calling it Ṭābah, at approximately Bukhari 1872. Confirm both numbers before use.

**On calling Madinah a cure.** The refrain uses poetic hyperbole, not a claim of independent healing power. Bukhari records the Prophet's duʿāʾ asking Allah to make Madinah beloved to us, at approximately Bukhari 1889, and the hadith that Madinah expels its impurity as a furnace expels dross, at approximately Bukhari 1883. The poem's imagery draws on this material.

**On the wish to visit.** The third stanza requests travel to the Prophet's mosque to pray, remember Allah and recite Qurʾān. Islamweb has issued a fatwa (number 106750) confirming that this wording contains nothing that contradicts ʿaqīdah, since the stated objects of the journey are prayer and dhikr in the mosque. This aligns the text with the standard Shāfiʿī position on ziyārah.

**On the omitted stanzas.** The verses addressing ʿAlī, al-Hasan and al-Husayn that appear in your original text are not heterodox in themselves, since the merit of the Prophet's family is authenticated. I removed them because you asked for the recension that Sunni communities transmit as standard. If you want them restored, the Ali Elsayed recension is the safer choice, because it places the four rightly guided caliphs in sequence before the Ahl al-Bayt stanza.
