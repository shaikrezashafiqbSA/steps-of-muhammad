# Duʿāʾ for Righteous Offspring — Calling Upon Allāh's Beautiful Names

These are the Quranic supplications, prophetic invocations, and divine names to call upon when asking Allāh for children, their protection, and their guidance — sourced from the Qurʾān, authenticated ḥadīth, and the stories of the Prophets.

---

*The supplication of Prophet Zakariyyā ʿalayhi al-salām*

### Translation

> "My Lord, grant me from Yourself righteous offspring. Indeed, You are the Hearer of supplication." *(Qurʾān 3:38)*

### Translation

> "My Lord, grant me [a child] from the righteous." *(Qurʾān 37:100)*

## The Beautiful Names of Allāh for This Duʿāʾ

### 1. Al-Khāliq (الخالق) — The Creator

**Primary Quranic Evidence:**

*"He is Allāh, the Creator (al-Khāliq), the Inventor, the Fashioner; to Him belong the most beautiful names."* (Qurʾān 59:24)

*"That is Allāh, your Lord; there is no deity except Him, the Creator (Khāliq) of all things, so worship Him."* (Qurʾān 6:102)

**When to invoke:** When struggling with infertility or any circumstance where a child seems impossible — for creation belongs entirely to Al-Khāliq.

**Duʿāʾ:**
> Yā Khāliq, create for me righteous offspring and bless my family.

---

### 2. Al-Wahhāb (الوهاب) — The Bestower of Gifts

**Primary Quranic Evidence — the Name Zakariyyā ʿa.s. invoked:**

[[رَبَّنَا|Our Lord|Rabbanā]] [[لَا|do not|lā]] [[تُزِغْ|let deviate|tuzigh]] [[قُلُوبَنَا|our hearts|qulūbanā]] [[بَعْدَ|after|baʿda]] [[إِذْ|when|idh]] [[هَدَيْتَنَا|You guided us|hadaytanā]] [[وَهَبْ|and grant|wa-hab]] [[لَنَا|to us|lanā]] [[مِن|from|min]] [[لَّدُنكَ|Yourself|ladunka]] [[رَحْمَةً|mercy|raḥmah]] [[إِنَّكَ|indeed You|innaka]] [[أَنتَ|are|anta]] [[الْوَهَّابُ|the Bestower|l-Wahhāb]]

> "Our Lord, do not let our hearts deviate after You have guided us, and grant us from Yourself mercy. Indeed, You are the Bestower (al-Wahhāb)." *(Qurʾān 3:8)*

**Further Evidence:**

*"Or do they possess the treasuries of your Lord's mercy, the Almighty, the Bestower (al-Wahhāb)?"* (Qurʾān 38:9)

*"[Zakariyyā said:] 'This is a gift (waḥbah) from You.'"* (Qurʾān 3:37 — context before 3:38)

**Note:** Zakariyyā ʿa.s. witnessed Allāh providing Maryam with out-of-season fruit (Qurʾān 3:37). Recognising that al-Wahhāb gives without restriction, he immediately turned to Allāh and made this duʿāʾ for a child.

**Duʿāʾ:**
> Yā Wahhāb, grant me righteous offspring as a gift from You.

---

### 3. Ar-Razzāq (الرزاق) — The Provider

**Primary Quranic Evidence:**

*"Indeed, it is Allāh who is the continual Provider (al-Razzāq), the firm possessor of strength."* (Qurʾān 51:58)

*"And there is no creature on earth but that upon Allāh is its provision (rizquhu)."* (Qurʾān 11:6)

**Duʿāʾ:**
> Yā Razzāq, bless me with righteous offspring from Your bounty.

---

### 4. Al-Qādir (القادر) — The All-Powerful

**Primary Quranic Evidence:**

*"Is not He who created the heavens and the earth able (bi-qādir) to create the likes of them? Yes, [it is so]; and He is the Knowing Creator. His command is only when He intends a thing that He says to it, 'Be,' and it is."* (Qurʾān 36:81–82)

*"Indeed, He is over all things competent (qadīr)."* (Qurʾān 2:20)

**Prophetic Precedent:**

Allāh gave Zakariyyā ʿa.s. a son despite his extreme old age and his wife's barrenness:

> *"O Zakariyyā, indeed We give you good tidings of a boy whose name will be Yaḥyā. We have not assigned to any before this name. He said: My Lord, how will I have a boy when my wife has been barren and I have reached extreme old age?"* (Qurʾān 19:7–8)

Allāh gave Ibrāhīm ʿa.s. children in old age:

> *"Praise to Allāh who has granted me, in old age, Ismāʿīl and Isḥāq. Indeed, my Lord is the Hearer of supplication."* (Qurʾān 14:39)

Both are proofs that Al-Qādir is constrained by nothing — no age, no medical condition, no worldly impossibility.

**Duʿāʾ:**
> Yā Qādir, by Your power, bless me with a righteous child.

---

### 5. Al-Mujīb (المجيب) — The One Who Responds

**Primary Quranic Evidence:**

*"And when My servants ask you concerning Me — indeed I am near. I respond to the call of the caller (ajību daʿwat al-dāʿ) when he calls upon Me."* (Qurʾān 2:186)

*"Indeed, my Lord is near and responsive (mujīb)."* (Qurʾān 11:61) — the words of Prophet Ṣāliḥ ʿa.s.

*"And [mention] Ayyūb, when he called to his Lord: 'Adversity has touched me, and You are the Most Merciful of the merciful.' So We responded to him (fa-istajabna lah)."* (Qurʾān 21:83–84)

**When to invoke:** When you have been making duʿāʾ for a long time and feel unheard — for Allāh is al-Mujīb, and He hears every sincere supplication.

**Duʿāʾ:**
> Yā Mujīb, answer my duʿāʾ and bless me with righteous offspring.

---

### 6. Al-Ḥafīẓ (الحفيظ) — The Protector

**Primary Quranic Evidence:**

*"But Allāh is the best guardian (khayr ḥāfiẓan), and He is the most merciful of the merciful."* (Qurʾān 12:64)

This is the statement of Yaʿqūb ʿa.s. when entrusting his sons — including Yūsuf — to Allāh's protection. It is the Quranic model for a parent placing their children under Allāh's care.

*"Indeed, my Lord is Guardian (ḥafīẓ) over all things."* (Qurʾān 11:57)

*"And He is the subjugator over His servants, and He sends over you guardian-angels (ḥafaẓah) until, when death comes to one of you, Our messengers take him."* (Qurʾān 6:61)

**Duʿāʾ:**
> Yā Ḥafīẓ, protect my children in their religion, health, character, and future.

---

### 7. Al-Hādī (الهادي) — The Guide

**Primary Quranic Evidence:**

*"And indeed, Allāh is the Guide (la-hādī) of those who have believed to a straight path."* (Qurʾān 22:54)

*"And your Lord is sufficient as a Guide (hādī) and a Helper."* (Qurʾān 25:31)

**Prophetic Context:**

Among the most beloved of a parent's duas is that their children be guided. The Qurʾān records Ibrāhīm ʿa.s. supplicating:

> *"My Lord, make me an establisher of prayer, and [many] from my descendants. Our Lord, and accept my supplication."* (Qurʾān 14:40)

**Duʿāʾ:**
> Yā Hādī, guide my children to what pleases You and make them among the righteous.

---

### 8 & 9. Al-Muqaddim (المقدم) & Al-Muʾakhkhir (المؤخر) — The One Who Brings Forward & The One Who Delays

**These two names are confirmed together in a Ṣaḥīḥ hadith — the night-prayer duʿāʾ of the Prophet ﷺ:**

> *"...Forgive me what I have sent forward and what I have left behind, what I have concealed and what I have declared. **You are Al-Muqaddim (the One who brings forward) and You are Al-Muʾakhkhir (the One who delays)**. There is no god but You."*
>
> *(Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī, no. 1120; Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, no. 769 — narrated by Ibn ʿAbbās raḍiy Allāhu ʿanhu)*

**Grade: Ṣaḥīḥ** — narrated in both Bukhārī and Muslim; the highest level of authentication.

**Understanding the pair:**

- **Al-Muqaddim** — Allāh brings things forward according to His wisdom. Sometimes what you await arrives sooner than expected, because He commands it forward.
- **Al-Muʾakhkhir** — Allāh delays not because He has forgotten you, but because He knows the perfect time. *"Perhaps you dislike a thing and Allāh makes therein much good."* (Qurʾān 4:19)

**Duʿāʾ:**
> Yā Muqaddim, bring this blessing forward and make it easy for me.
>
> Yā Muʾakhkhir, make me pleased with Your timing and grant me patience until what You have decreed for me arrives.

---

## Combined Duʿāʾ

*A composed supplication (muṣannaf) drawing upon the authentic Quranic names and themes above. The divine names are all Quranic; the phrase "coolness of my eyes" (qurrata aʿyunin) is taken directly from Qurʾān 25:74.*

> **Yā Wahhāb, Yā Razzāq, Yā Khāliq, Yā Qādir, Yā Mujīb, Yā Raḥmān, Yā Muqaddim, wa Yā Muʾakhkhir** — bless me with righteous offspring who will be the coolness of my eyes, a source of goodness in this world, and a means of reward in the Hereafter.
>
> Yā Allāh, bring this blessing forward by Your mercy. And if You delay it by Your wisdom, make me patient, content with Your decree, and steadfast in my hope in You. Indeed, You are the Most Generous, the Most Wise.

---

## Notes & Context

### The Commandment to Call Upon Allāh by His Names

*"And to Allāh belong the most beautiful names (al-asmāʾ al-ḥusnā), so invoke Him by them."* (Qurʾān 7:180)

Ibn al-Qayyim al-Jawziyyah explains in *al-Wābil al-Ṣayyib* that among the highest forms of duʿāʾ is to call upon Allāh by the name that most corresponds to the nature of one's need. Asking for a child while invoking Al-Wahhāb acknowledges it as a gift. Invoking Al-Qādir acknowledges that nothing is beyond Allāh's power. This is the Prophetic and Quranic method.

### The Story of Zakariyyā ʿalayhi al-salām

Zakariyyā ʿa.s. was an elderly prophet with a barren wife. He witnessed Allāh providing Maryam with out-of-season fruit in her prayer chamber and, recognising that al-Wahhāb, al-Razzāq and al-Qādir have no limits, immediately made duʿāʾ for a child. The full account spans Sūrat Āl ʿImrān (3:37–41) and Sūrat Maryam (19:2–11). His supplication was answered with the birth of Yaḥyā ʿa.s. — despite every worldly obstacle — as a proof for all believers.

### The Story of Ibrāhīm ʿalayhi al-salām

Ibrāhīm ʿa.s. was given Ismāʿīl and Isḥāq in old age, and responded with gratitude: *"Praise to Allāh who has granted me in old age Ismāʿīl and Isḥāq. Indeed, my Lord is the Hearer of supplication."* (Qurʾān 14:39). His earlier duʿāʾ (Qurʾān 37:100) — "My Lord, grant me from the righteous" — is a reminder that duʿāʾ for a child may be separated from its answer by years, yet Allāh's timing has never been wrong.

### On Combining Al-Muqaddim and Al-Muʾakhkhir

These two names are never separated in the Prophetic tradition. The Prophet ﷺ mentioned them together in his nightly duʿāʾ (Bukhārī 1120), teaching that Allāh's command over timing — whether to hasten or delay — is total and perfect. A person waiting for a child should hold both names in heart: asking Al-Muqaddim to hasten the blessing, while trusting Al-Muʾakhkhir's wisdom if the answer is delayed.

<summary>Full Arabic Text of the Night-Prayer Duʿāʾ Confirming Al-Muqaddim & Al-Muʾakhkhir (Bukhārī 1120)</summary>

Narrated by Ibn ʿAbbās (raḍiy Allāhu ʿanhumā): When the Prophet ﷺ would rise for the night prayer, he would say:

> اللَّهُمَّ لَكَ الْحَمْدُ أَنْتَ قَيِّمُ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالأَرْضِ وَمَنْ فِيهِنَّ، وَلَكَ الْحَمْدُ لَكَ مُلْكُ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالأَرْضِ وَمَنْ فِيهِنَّ، وَلَكَ الْحَمْدُ أَنْتَ نُورُ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالأَرْضِ وَمَنْ فِيهِنَّ، وَلَكَ الْحَمْدُ أَنْتَ الْحَقُّ، وَوَعْدُكَ الْحَقُّ، وَلِقَاؤُكَ حَقٌّ، وَقَوْلُكَ حَقٌّ، وَالْجَنَّةُ حَقٌّ، وَالنَّارُ حَقٌّ، وَالنَّبِيُّونَ حَقٌّ، وَمُحَمَّدٌ حَقٌّ، وَالسَّاعَةُ حَقٌّ. اللَّهُمَّ لَكَ أَسْلَمْتُ، وَبِكَ آمَنْتُ، وَعَلَيْكَ تَوَكَّلْتُ، وَإِلَيْكَ أَنَبْتُ، وَبِكَ خَاصَمْتُ، وَإِلَيْكَ حَاكَمْتُ. فَاغْفِرْ لِي مَا قَدَّمْتُ وَمَا أَخَّرْتُ، وَمَا أَسْرَرْتُ وَمَا أَعْلَنْتُ، **أَنْتَ الْمُقَدِّمُ وَأَنْتَ الْمُؤَخِّرُ**، لاَ إِلَهَ إِلاَّ أَنْتَ.

*"O Allāh, all praise is Yours. You are the Sustainer of the heavens and the earth and all that is in them. All praise is Yours. Yours is the dominion of the heavens and the earth and all that is in them. All praise is Yours. You are the Light of the heavens and the earth and all that is in them. All praise is Yours. You are the Truth, Your promise is truth, Your meeting is truth, Your word is truth, Paradise is true, the Fire is true, the Prophets are true, Muḥammad is true, and the Hour is true. O Allāh, to You I have submitted, in You I have believed, upon You I have relied, to You I have turned, by Your help I have contended, and to You I bring my disputes. So forgive me what I have sent forward and what I have left behind, what I have concealed and what I have declared. **You are Al-Muqaddim and You are Al-Muʾakhkhir. There is no god but You."***

*(Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī, no. 1120; Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, no. 769)*

<details>
<summary>Scholarly Note: Status of the 99-Names Enumerative List (Tirmidhī 3507)</summary>

**The Concept (Mutawātir in Meaning):**
The ḥadīth establishing that Allāh has 99 names is narrated in Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī (no. 2736) and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim (no. 2677) and is of the highest authenticity.

**The Specific List in Tirmidhī:**
The enumerative list of all 99 names — which includes al-Muqaddim and al-Muʾakhkhir — appears in Sunan al-Tirmidhī (no. 3507), narrated through Walīd ibn Muslim from Shuʿayb ibn Abī Ḥamza. Al-Tirmidhī himself graded it *ḥasan gharīb*. Ibn Ḥajar al-ʿAsqalānī notes in *Fatḥ al-Bārī* that the specific enumeration may have originated with a narrator (Walīd ibn Muslim) rather than directly from the Prophet ﷺ. Imām al-Bayhaqī raised similar reservations in *al-Asmāʾ wa al-Ṣifāt*.

**Conclusion:**
The names **Al-Muqaddim** and **Al-Muʾakhkhir** are not dependent on the Tirmidhī list. They are established beyond doubt by the Bukhārī/Muslim night-prayer ḥadīth, where the Prophet ﷺ explicitly used both names in his own supplication. This is the stronger and more direct evidence.

</details>

<summary>Full Story of Zakariyyā ʿa.s. — Sūrat Maryam (19:2–11)</summary>

> *"[This is] a mention of the mercy of your Lord to His servant Zakariyyā — when he called to his Lord a private supplication. >
> *"[He was told:] 'O Zakariyyā, indeed We give you good tidings of a boy whose name will be Yaḥyā. We have not assigned to any before this name.' [The angel] said: 'Thus [it will be]; your Lord says, "It is easy for Me, for I created you before, while you were nothing."'"* *(Qurʾān 19:2–9)*

This passage illustrates Al-Qādir, Al-Wahhāb, Al-Mujīb, and Al-Muqaddim all in one narrative.
